As a software developer, I strongly believe that good software is not just about security and functionality; it is also about how easily users can accomplish their goals.

Recently, I experienced this firsthand through my online banking experience as an NRI (Non-Resident Indian).

## My experience with Canara Bank

As an NRI living abroad, I usually access net banking approximately once every two months. However, whenever I return to use the service, Canara Bank asks me to re-enter details such as my IFSC code, account number, and Aadhaar number as part of the additional verification process.

I understand that banking is a sensitive domain and that security is essential. However, repeatedly providing these details makes a routine banking activity frustrating.

The problem became even more challenging when I tried to open a new fixed deposit. Despite submitting the requested information, I could not complete the login process and carry out the transaction as expected.

Eventually, after facing these difficulties repeatedly, I decided to move my banking activities to SBI.

## My experience with SBI

Compared with my experience with Canara Bank, I found SBI's online banking experience smoother and more convenient for my needs. Completing routine banking activities with fewer obstacles made a noticeable difference.

This experience reminded me how much the quality of a digital service affects customer satisfaction.

## What can software developers learn from this?

### 1. Security should not come at the expense of usability.

Security is essential, but authentication workflows should be designed to protect legitimate users while minimizing unnecessary friction.

### 2. Understand different user scenarios.

An NRI who accesses banking services occasionally has different needs from someone who logs in every day. Product design should consider these usage patterns.

### 3. Make routine tasks simple.

Customers should be able to complete common activities, such as checking their accounts or opening a fixed deposit, without getting stuck in repeated verification loops.

### 4. User experience directly affects customer retention.

Customers may tolerate occasional inconvenience, but repeated friction can eventually lead them to choose an alternative service.

## My takeaway

As software developers, architects, and product designers, we should remember that a system can be secure, technically sound, and feature-rich, yet still deliver a poor user experience.

Users don't experience our architecture or security design directly. They experience the workflows we create for them.

The real challenge is to build systems that are secure, reliable, and easy to use.

Because in the end, great software doesn't just protect users. It helps them accomplish their goals with confidence and ease.
